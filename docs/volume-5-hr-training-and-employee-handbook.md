## 1. Introduction
Section 2 is the Employee Handbook — the one part every employee actually receives and acknowledges. Everything after it is the CEO/HR Lead's operating detail for delivering on what the handbook promises.

> **⚠️ This volume does not cover the launch workforce — open item O5, currently blocking.**
> Volume 1 §9.1 runs the business on **subcontractors for the first 6–12 months**. Every program in this volume — induction (§3), core training (§4.1), certification tracking (§4.3), the buddy system (§3.4), the performance framework (§6) — is employee-only. As written, **this volume delivers nothing usable for the entire first year of trading.**
>
> Meanwhile Volume 2 §2.12 requires subcontractors to perform "to the same CLN and QUA SOP standards as employees," and QUA-01 inspects them against those standards. There is currently no documented way to bring a subcontractor up to those standards, verify they understand them, or record that it happened.
>
> **A Subcontractor Engagement & Standards Pack is required before the first subcontractor works a job.** Section 7 below specifies what it must contain. It is deliberately *not* an induction program — see the caution there, because how this is written interacts directly with the classification risk in Volume 2 §2.12.
>
> **Every role named in this volume is also unstaffed at launch.** HR Lead, Finance Lead and Cleaning Supervisor own most steps in §3 and §4; none activates until the Scale stage. Volume 2 §3.1 now names interim owners for every SOP category — the same principle applies here: the CEO holds all of it until delegated.

## 2. Employee Handbook
Distributed to and acknowledged by every employee at induction. Subcontractors receive the Subcontractor Agreement instead — their relationship is governed differently, including the $50/hour cleaner rate (Volume 1, Section 9.1).

### 2.1 Welcome
Welcome to Vantage Point Cleaning. We built this business on a simple idea: you can prove home care quality with data instead of just promising it. Every person on the team makes that promise real in a client's home somewhere today.

### 2.2 Who We Are
Vantage Point Cleaning provides premium recurring residential home care across the Gold Coast. Our mission, vision, and values are set out in Volume 1 — every new hire is walked through these at induction.

### 2.3 Employment Basics
- Most cleaning roles are covered by the Cleaning Services Award 2020 (MA000022); your offer letter states your classification level and employment type.
- You will receive the Fair Work Information Statement at or before your start date.
- Ordinary hours, rostering, and penalty rates follow the current Award rate table.
- Casual employees are paid casual loading in lieu of most leave entitlements; part-time and full-time employees accrue leave per the Fair Work National Employment Standards.

### 2.4 Code of Conduct
- Represent the brand professionally in every home — uniform worn correctly, punctual arrival, respectful communication.
- Client keys, alarm codes, and access details are used only for the scheduled job and never shared, duplicated, or photographed.
- Report any damage, breakage, or incident immediately — hiding an incident is treated more seriously than the incident itself.
- Zero tolerance for harassment, discrimination, or bullying, consistent with the Fair Work Act and Anti-Discrimination Act.
- Chemicals and equipment are used strictly per the dilution standard and relevant Safety Data Sheet — never mixed or substituted informally, and never a client's own product without confirming it first.
- Client privacy in the home is respected at all times — no photographing or discussing the interior of a client's home outside of required inspection documentation.

### 2.5 Work Health & Safety
You have both a right and a responsibility here. Vantage Point provides a safe system of work, correct PPE, and training; you're responsible for following it, reporting hazards, and never working unsafely. Read the master emergency response sequence at induction, before your first shift.

### 2.6 Leave & Absence
- Notify your Cleaning Supervisor or the roster contact as early as possible for any absence — recurring home visits depend on coverage, and last-minute notice affects a client's trust in the service.
- Leave requests follow the standard leave process, submitted through Employment Hero where available.
- Unauthorised or unexplained absence is addressed through the disciplinary process (Section 6.4), not assumed to be resignation.

### 2.7 Uniform & Presentation
Uniform and PPE are issued at induction and must be worn on every job, kept clean, and reported for replacement when worn.

### 2.8 Technology, Privacy & Social Media
- Company accounts and apps are for business use.
- Never post client home photos, addresses, or identifying details on personal social media without written company approval.
- Your personal information is handled per Privacy Act obligations — you can request to see what's held about you at any time.

### 2.9 Pay & Incentives
Full detail is in Section 5. In short: you're paid correctly and on time per the Award, and there are structured ways to earn more than base rate through the incentive programs.

### 2.10 Raising a Concern
Talk to your Cleaning Supervisor first for most issues. For anything involving your supervisor, go directly to the CEO or HR Lead. The grievance process is documented in Section 6.5.

### 2.11 Ending Employment
- **Resignation:** provide notice per your employment contract/Award minimum, in writing, to your Cleaning Supervisor or HR Lead.
- **Termination:** follows the disciplinary process (Section 6.4) except in cases of serious misconduct.
- **Exit process** includes access revocation (including any client-home keys/codes issued) and final pay.

### 2.12 Acknowledgement
Every employee signs an acknowledgement that they have received, read, and understood this handbook at induction.

## 3. Onboarding & Induction Program
Runs from offer acceptance to 30 days.

### 3.1 Day One
*Owners below are end-state roles. Until each activates, the CEO holds the step (Volume 2, Section 3.1).*

| Item | Owner |
|---|---|
| Welcome and company overview | HR Lead |
| Employee Handbook distribution and acknowledgement | HR Lead |
| Uniform and PPE issue | HR Lead / Cleaning Supervisor |
| System access provisioning (email, Connecteam) | CEO / HR Lead |
| Payroll and super details confirmed | Finance Lead |
| Fair Work Information Statement issued (if not already at offer) | HR Lead |
| WHS induction — master response sequence, in-home hazards | Cleaning Supervisor |

### 3.2 First Week
- Shadow shift(s) with an experienced crew member or the Cleaning Supervisor before any unsupervised job.
- Walkthrough of the relevant service-tier cleaning SOPs (Essential or Signature at launch; Estate Care only once that tier opens) for the homes they'll be assigned to. **Note CLN-21 and CLN-22 must exist first — open item O6.**
- SafetyCulture checklist training — completing an inspection accurately and honestly, not just quickly.
- Confirm first pay run processed correctly — a deliberate trust-building check in week one.

### 3.3 First 30 Days
- 30-day check-in with HR Lead or CEO — informal, focused on how the role is landing.
- Confirm training record logged and any outstanding certification scheduled.
- Formal induction is marked complete only once every Day One/First Week item is signed off.

### 3.4 Buddy System
Every new employee is paired with an experienced team member for their first two weeks — a light-touch pairing, not a formal mentorship program.

### 5.1 Base Pay
Every cleaning role is paid at or above the applicable Cleaning Services Award 2020 (MA000022) rate for their classification, reviewed against the Annual Wage Review each 1 July. Subcontractors are paid at $50/hour per Volume 1, Section 9.1 — above the Award band, and note the classification warning recorded there. ### 5.2 Vehicle & Expense Allowances
Staff using a personal vehicle for work travel between homes are paid a per-kilometre allowance, funded from operating expenses.

| ~~Verified-review incentive (subcontractors)~~ | **⚠️ On hold — open items O1 and O4.** Volume 1 §9.1 proposes maintaining subcontractor quality through incentives tied to verified Google reviews. ## 6. Performance Management Framework

### 6.1 Review Cycle
- Probation check-in at 30 days — informal.
- Formal performance review at 90 days, then every six months.
- Every review is measured against the specific KPIs defined for that role (Volume 2) — not a generic scorecard.

### 6.2 What a Good Review Looks Like
- Concrete evidence, not impressions — inspection trend, attendance record, client feedback, KPI performance.
- Two-way conversation — the employee's own view is recorded, not just management's assessment.
- A written outcome and, where relevant, a development or improvement plan with a follow-up date.

### 6.3 Career Pathways
Cleaner → Cleaning Supervisor → Operations Manager is the primary internal pathway, deliberately kept open and communicated.

### 6.4 Disciplinary Process
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Informal conversation | For a first, minor issue — documented briefly, no formal warning. |
| Formal verbal warning | For a repeated or more serious issue — documented, chance to respond given. |
| Written warning | For continued or serious issues — clear expectation and timeframe given. |
| Final written warning | Last step before termination — HR Lead and CEO both involved. |
| Termination | Only after due process above, or immediately for serious misconduct. |

Every step is documented and filed in the employee's record.

### 6.5 Grievance Handling
Any employee grievance is heard promptly, taken seriously, and never held against the employee for having raised it.

## 7. Subcontractor Engagement & Standards Pack — Specification
**Status: to be written. Blocking — no subcontractor should work a job before this exists.** Owner: CEO (open item O5).

Sections 2–6 above govern employees. This section specifies what governs the launch workforce, which is entirely subcontracted (Volume 1 §9.1). It is a **specification for a document that does not yet exist**, not the document itself.

### 7.1 Why this is not an induction program
Read Volume 2 §2.12 first. A subcontractor is engaged as an independent business, and the more the engagement resembles employee onboarding — company training on company methods, supervision, uniforms, rostering — the weaker the independence position becomes. That tension is unresolved (open item O1) and needs legal advice.

The design principle that keeps both sides workable: **specify outcomes and standards, not methods and hours.** A subcontractor is told what "clean" means at Vantage Point and how it will be verified; they are not told how to hold a cloth or when to take a break. Anything in this pack that reads as instruction on *how* to work should be challenged before it goes in.

### 7.2 What the pack must contain
| Component | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Subcontractor Agreement | The engagement itself | Lawpath-drafted (Volume 1 §3.9). Must state the rate basis, the right to delegate, and the absence of an employment relationship. **Note Vol 1 §9.1's warning** — an hourly rate weakens the independence position and needs legal review before this template is used. |
| Compliance document set | Gate before any job | The four COM-02 documents: $10M Certificate of Currency naming their exact ABN business name, active ABN, signed agreement, current police check. Non-negotiable — no keys or access codes are issued until all four verify. |
| **Service standard specifications** | What "done" means | CLN-21 (Essential) and CLN-22 (Signature) once written — open item O6. These are the deliverable specification, provided as a standard to meet rather than a method to follow. |
| Quality verification standard | How work is checked | The QUA-01 SafetyCulture checklist, provided up front so the standard is known before the first job rather than discovered at inspection. |
| Site and safety information | WHS duties | Chemical SDS access (COM-01), incident reporting path (EMR-01), and the in-home conduct expectations that protect the client. A principal contractor retains WHS duties regardless of engagement type — confirm scope with the WHS adviser. |
| Client-facing conduct standard | Brand and privacy | Key/code handling, privacy in the home, no photography beyond inspection documentation. Mirrors §2.4 because the obligation is owed to the client, not to the employment relationship. |
| Access protocol | Practical | Feeds OPS-07. |
| Payment and invoicing terms | Commercial clarity | How the split is calculated, when invoices are issued, TPAR implications (FIN-09). |

### 7.3 Verification, not training
Rather than a shadowed induction, engagement runs through REC-01's **paid, time-boxed trial job**, reviewed against the CLN service standard and a QUA-01 inspection. That is a competence check on an independent business, which sits far better with the engagement model than a training program does.

### 7.4 The pricing floor
Volume 1 §9.2 sets a per-visit floor below which the 50% split stops producing a defensible effective hourly rate. Applying it is part of engagement, not an afterthought: a subcontractor accepting work below that rate is evidence against the independence position, not merely a cheap job.

### 7.5 What does not apply to subcontractors
The Employee Handbook (§2), induction (§3), the core training program (§4.1), certification tracking (§4.3), the buddy system (§3.4), the performance and disciplinary framework (§6), leave, and the Award pay provisions in §5.1. Applying any of these to a subcontractor undermines the engagement model. Where a genuine problem arises, the remedy is contractual — under the Subcontractor Agreement — not disciplinary.

**Note on §5.3's verified-review incentive.** Volume 5 §5.3 currently ties subcontractor incentive payments to verified Google reviews. That is both an indicium of control and part of the review-gating exposure flagged as open item O4. Resolve both before the incentive is offered.
