# Vantage Point Cleaning — Landing Page Content Brief
## Home Care · Luxury Homes · Estates · Turnover
Companion to Residential Volume 3, Section 4 (Funnel Library)

---

## Why four pages, not one
Competitor review confirmed nobody in Zones 1–5 runs genuinely separated pages for these buyer segments — even The Gold Standard Co., the closest positioning match, mixes luxury residential, boutique commercial, and Airbnb turnover under one voice. Each segment below is comparing you against a different set of alternatives and needs different proof to convert. Mixing them dilutes the price anchor (a $180 clean offer sitting next to Estate Care content undercuts it) and sends the wrong nurture content to the wrong lead.

---

## 1. Home Care Page (Essential / Signature tiers)

**Who lands here:** time-poor professionals and families in Zones 1–5, comparison-shopping against Home Style Cleaning, Simply Maid, Gold Coast Cleans, Calibre Cleaning.

**Headline direction:** lead with time and trust, not the clean itself. e.g. *"Your home, always ready — without you managing a thing."* Avoid "cleaning service" language in the H1; competitors already own that phrase.

**Above the fold:**
- Headline + one-line subhead naming the actual product: a managed recurring plan, not a booking.
- CTA: **"Request Your Private Home Care Assessment"** (per Volume 1, Section 5.3 — never "Get a Quote").
- Zone-aware trust line if feasible (e.g. "Now servicing Burleigh to Broadbeach" dynamically by zone, or a simple 5-zone map graphic).

**Proof points (in order):**
1. Consistency — same cleaner/team every visit (a real differentiator vs. Simply Maid/franchise churn).
2. Verified quality — reference the SafetyCulture inspection standard in plain language: "every clean is checked against a documented standard, not just hoped for." This is the single biggest gap found in every competitor reviewed — none publish any form of verifiable quality data.
3. 3Rs review program — real, recent client feedback (Section 3.2 of Volume 3).
4. Essential vs. Signature comparison table — plain-language, not hourly-rate based (Volume 1, Section 2.6: price is set by scope and value, not bedroom count — don't put a $/hr number on this page at all).

**Objection handling:** a short FAQ addressing "why not just book an hourly cleaner" — frame around reliability and presentation standard, not price.

**Form (Volume 3, Section 5.1):** the instant quoter — bedrooms, bathrooms, powder rooms, living areas, tier, frequency, condition rating, plus the increment questions. Returns a published package price. Mandatory timestamped T&Cs acknowledgement, plus a Privacy Collection Notice at submission (Volume 3, Section 5.8).

**GoHighLevel tag:** `home-care` → feeds standard Residential pipeline (Volume 3, Section 2.1).

## 2. Luxury Homes Page

**Who lands here:** higher-ticket homeowners in larger properties, Zones 2, 4, and 5 especially (Palm Beach canal-front, Main Beach/Surfers high-rise, Hope Island waterfront) — the buyer Land Based Cleaning and Steam Boss are currently talking to.

**Headline direction:** presentation and discretion, not price or convenience. e.g. *"A standard of care that matches your home."*

**Above the fold:**
- CTA: still **"Request Your Private Home Care Assessment"** but the surrounding copy should read like an invitation, not a form — this buyer is used to being courted, not funneled.
- No "book online in minutes" language anywhere on this page — that's the Absolute Domestics/Simply Maid playbook, and the competitor analysis classifies both as Value-quadrant. This applies to pricing too: no estimate, no range, no figure before the assessment.
- **A published price and a booking, framed as an invitation rather than a transaction.** Luxury Homes uses the same ladder as Home Care at the Signature tier, but the surrounding copy should read as an invitation — the price is stated plainly and once, not merchandised. No countdowns, no "from" pricing, no discount language.

**Proof points:**
1. Signature Home Care service description in full — linen, laundry, interior glass, detailed kitchen, rotational deep-clean tasks (Volume 1, Section 2.3).
2. Discretion and professionalism — brief mention of vetting/police checks, framed as protecting the client's home, not as a compliance checkbox.
3. A short "what makes this different" block naming inconsistency as the real cost of the alternative (this is directly the angle The Gold Standard Co. uses in their content marketing — worth matching, since it clearly resonates with this buyer).
4. Photography matters most here — commission real (or high-quality stock in the interim) imagery of presentation-standard interiors; this page will underperform on stock generic-cleaner imagery.
5. Brief, plain-language note near the price: it is fixed for the scope described, with the variance clause explained in one sentence rather than buried — *"if your home turns out to be larger than described, we'll talk to you before we do anything extra."* Pre-empting the "why wasn't this the final price" objection is worth more than hiding it in the T&Cs.

**Form:** the short assessment-request form (Volume 3, Section 5.1), plus an optional "tell us about your property" free-text field — this buyer expects to be asked, not just processed. Detailed property capture happens on site (Volume 3, Section 5.1a). Assessment booking uses the same calendar pattern as the Estates consultation flow.

**GoHighLevel tag:** `luxury-homes` → Residential pipeline, tagged for Signature-tier follow-up sequencing (higher-touch, slower cadence than Essential-tier nudges); walkthrough bookings tagged separately so Sales can prioritise their calendar around them.

## 3. Estates Page

**Who lands here:** the #2 high-value niche (Volume 1, Section 2.2) — large/high-value homes wanting a dedicated team, comparing you against private estate managers and concierge property services, not "house cleaners."

**Headline direction:** consultation-first, not transactional. e.g. *"Dedicated home care for exceptional properties."*

**Above the fold:**
- CTA: **"Request a Private Consultation"** — deliberately different wording from the other three pages, so this segment self-identifies and never sees a generic quote form.
- No pricing bands shown publicly (Volume 1, Section 2.6 treats Estate Care as $1,500–3,000+ — internal reference only). A single reassuring line — *"We'll provide a preliminary estimate on enquiry, with your exact price confirmed during a private walkthrough"* — sets the right expectation without publishing a number.

**Proof points:**
1. Estate Care service description in full — dedicated team, detailed cleaning program, rotational maintenance, priority scheduling, supervisor inspection, concierge-style communication.
2. Supervisor-level quality assurance — name the fact that this tier gets direct QA Supervisor inspection (Volume 2, QUA-02 Client Home Walkthrough), not just a standard checklist.
3. A single, understated case-study-style testimonial if/when available — this page should feel curated, not full of reviews (volume of reviews reads as mass-market, which undercuts this tier).
4. The walkthrough itself as a proof point, not a formality — frame it as part of the service ("we don't price your home without seeing it") rather than an administrative step before booking.

**Form:** minimal — Name, Phone, Email, Suburb, and a free-text "About your property" field. No frequency/tier dropdown; this is handled in the consultation, not the form. An optional property size field can feed a rough preliminary estimate shown after submission, but always framed as subject to confirmation at the walkthrough.

**GoHighLevel tag:** `estates` → routed directly to CEO/Sales Manager task queue, not an automated instant-estimate flow (consistent with Volume 3, Section 2.1's "Non-Standard Request" handling — Estates is always walkthrough-quoted by design, not an exception).

## 4. Turnover Page (Property Managers Only)

**Who lands here:** premium property management companies and holiday-let operators — explicitly *not* individual Airbnb hosts (Volume 1, Section 2.5). This page needs to self-select the right buyer, since the wrong lead here (a single-property host) wastes sales time and pulls the brand toward the commodity turnover market you're deliberately avoiding.

**Headline direction:** B2B, operational language, not homeowner language. e.g. *"Professionally managed presentation and turnover services for premium holiday accommodation."*

**Above the fold:**
- CTA: **"Enquire About Property Management Partnership"** — deliberately gates against solo hosts; a host looking for a one-off Airbnb clean should bounce off this page, not convert on it.
- Open with who this is for: "For property managers and holiday accommodation operators overseeing luxury apartments, penthouses, waterfront homes, and premium holiday properties" — named exclusions do real work here.

**Proof points:**
1. Full "Vantage Point Stay" service description if that division exists yet (Volume 1, Section 2.5): cleaning, linen, consumables, damage reporting, photo verification, guest-ready certification.
2. Frame this explicitly as *portfolio* servicing, not single-property booking — property managers think in portfolios, not one-off jobs.
3. A short note on turnover reliability/speed without ever using "cheap" or "fast" language — this needs to avoid reading like the $80-studio-turnover segment entirely.

**Form:** Company name, Contact name, Phone, Email, Number/type of properties managed, Areas serviced. No individual-property intake fields at all — reinforces the portfolio framing and further discourages solo-host submissions.

**GoHighLevel tag:** `turnover-pm` → routed to a manual qualification step before any automation — Volume 1 is explicit this is a selective, supplementary channel (target: ~10% of portfolio mix), so this funnel should never auto-quote.

## Cross-Page Rules
- **No page cross-links to another segment's pricing or tier language** — a Home Care visitor shouldn't stumble into Estate Care pricing context and anchor low; an Estates visitor shouldn't see Home Care's tier comparison table and anchor high against their own instincts.
- **Reviews stay segment-specific.** A glowing 3-bed/2-bath review is proof for Home Care, not for Estates — don't reuse the same testimonial block across pages.
- **Every page keeps the same visual brand system** (colours, typography, logo treatment) — the differentiation is in language, proof, and CTA, not in looking like four different companies.
- **Essential and Signature show published package prices; Estates and Turnover show none.** The pricing mechanism now differentiates the pages: the Home Care and Luxury Homes pages carry a price table and an instant quoter, while Estates leads with a private consultation and Turnover with a partnership enquiry. See `residential-package-and-pricing-model.md`.
- **Where prices appear, they must be accompanied by what is included and what is not.** At 1.02–1.23× the market ceiling the number alone does not signal premium — the bounded inclusions do the work.
