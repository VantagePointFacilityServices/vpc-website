## 1. Executive Summary
Vantage Point Cleaning is a technology-enabled residential cleaning company on the Gold Coast, built around **premium recurring home care** rather than commodity or turnover cleaning. The business runs on a lean SaaS technology stack from day one, with a proprietary AI Business Operating System (AIOS) layered in as revenue grows (Volume 4).

**Target market:** premium, high-ticket residential and specialty cleaning — not the price-competitive mainstream market. The Indigenous procurement channel that this volume previously carried now sits with the commercial brand (Section 8), because its sales cycle and compliance path are institutional rather than residential.

The objective is to build Vantage Point Cleaning into a $1M+ Gold Coast residential business without becoming a commodity cleaner — competing on trust, consistency, and presentation rather than on price per hour.

**Financial goal:** $110,000 in Year 1 revenue as the planning case, scaling to $280,000 in Year 2, $600,000 in Year 3, $1,200,000 in Year 4, and $2,500,000 in Year 5 (Section 10.1). Year 1 is modelled bottom-up from Home Care-only economics (Section 10.2); Years 2–5 are targets that require their own bottom-up model as each segment gate clears, not forecasts.

**A note on the previous $250,000 Year 1 target.** It was retired in the August 2026 audit because it was unreachable under this plan's own segment restriction: the $3,500/month average plan value it depended on implies roughly $808 per visit at weekly cadence, which is Section 2.6's **Luxury** band — a segment Section 2.2a defers until 20 recurring Home Care customers. Reaching $250,000 inside Year 1 on Home Care pricing alone would require roughly 4.5 new plans every month sustained for twelve months, three times the pace Section 9.2 describes, with no acquisition plan in this suite capable of producing it.

**Workforce model:** launches with vetted, fully insured subcontractors for the first 6–12 months, maintaining quality through incentives rather than direct supervision, transitioning to direct employment once revenue is predictable enough to support fixed payroll (Section 9).

## 2. Residential Strategy & Service Model

### 2.1 Strategic Rationale — Premium Recurring Home Care, Not Turnover Cleaning
The business is built around premium recurring residential home care, with luxury homes and estates as the high-value niche, and holiday/short-stay cleaning used selectively as a supplementary revenue stream rather than the core.

The Gold Coast short-term rental market is substantial — approximately 10,862 active listings, 62% average occupancy, and a $240 average daily rate — so turnover demand is real. But that market is operationally intense and structurally rewards speed, availability, and cost over relationship and margin. Local marketplace data shows standard three-bedroom cleans around $130–$300 and Airbnb turnovers from roughly $80 (studio) to $240 (larger home with linen) — exactly the price-compressed territory the brand is built to avoid.

Vantage Point Cleaning = "Premium Home Care." The customer isn't buying three hours of cleaning. They're buying someone they trust in their home, consistency, reliability, a professionally managed service, beautiful presentation, time back, and peace of mind.

### 2.2a Launch Phasing — Home Care First
The table above is the mature-state segment mix (Section 2.4). At launch, Vantage Point Cleaning runs a **single-segment focus, not the full four-page architecture at once**:

- **Phase 1 (launch → 20 customers holding an active recurring Home Care plan):** Home Care only — Essential and Signature tiers (Section 2.3). Luxury Homes, Estates, and Turnover are deferred, not abandoned. This concentrates the founder's limited time (Section 6) and the crew's early training on proving one assessment-to-plan flow, one service standard, and one recurring-plan sales motion before adding complexity.
- **Why 20, not a revenue figure:** 20 customers on an active recurring Home Care plan is a genuine operational proof point — it's enough client volume to validate assessment-based pricing across a real range of property sizes and conditions (Scope & Variance Protection document), to have real 3Rs review data to put in front of a Luxury Homes prospect, and to justify the Cleaning Supervisor field-inspection role activating on schedule (Volume 2, Section 2.3) before a higher-touch, higher-stakes tier is opened.
- **When the gate realistically clears:** at the Section 9.2 acquisition pace with the Section 10.2 churn assumption, 20 active plans is reached around **Month 24**, not within Year 1. Clearing it inside Year 1 requires roughly 3 new plans per month. This is a pace decision, not a passive milestone — track it against the counter in Section 11.1.
- **Phase 2 (20+ active recurring Home Care plans):** Luxury Homes page and its assessment flow go live. Estates and Turnover remain gated further still (Section 2.2b).
- **Phase 3 (Luxury Homes proven — client count/mix to be set once Phase 2 data exists):** Estates (consultation-only) and Turnover (property-manager partnerships) open.

### 2.2b What "Deferred" Means Operationally
Deferred does not mean undocumented — the Luxury Homes, Estates, and Turnover landing pages, forms, and pipeline logic (Volume 3, Section 4) should still be **built** during the MVP phase (they're cheap to build once the Home Care assessment flow exists) but **not launched or marketed** until the Section 2.2a gate is cleared. This avoids a second build cycle later, while keeping the sales team, crew training, and marketing spend concentrated on one segment until it's proven.

### 2.3 Service Packaging — Sold as Managed Home Care, Not Hourly Cleaning
Inclusions are **bounded** — an inclusion without a stated limit cannot be sold at a flat rate. Full specification, including the rotational schedule and the excluded add-on list, is in `docs/residential-package-and-pricing-model.md` §9.

- **Vantage Point Essential Home Care** (weekly/fortnightly), **$199–639**: kitchen (benches, splashback, sink, cooktop, appliance exteriors, microwave interior), bathrooms, all floors, dusting of reachable surfaces, beds made (linen not changed), bins, high-touch sanitising, consistent cleaner/team, SafetyCulture inspection logged, before-and-after photographs.
- **Vantage Point Signature Home Care**, **$349–879**: Essential plus linen changed on all beds (client-supplied), **one** laundry load, interior glass at reachable height, detailed kitchen, **one** rotational deep-clean task per visit from a published eight-visit cycle, consumable monitoring, priority booking window.
- **Vantage Point Estate Care** (larger/high-value homes) — **Phase 3, not launched at MVP (Section 2.2a):** dedicated team, detailed cleaning program, rotational maintenance, interior glass, detailed bathrooms, kitchen detailing, laundry, linen, home presentation, priority scheduling, supervisor inspection, concierge-style communication.

### 2.4 Portfolio Mix Target (Mature State)
70% premium recurring home care (the engine) · 20% luxury estates (high-ticket/high-margin) · 10% strategic short-stay (utilisation and cash flow only) — deliberately not an Airbnb-heavy mix, which would pull the brand toward commodity cleaning. **This is the target mix once all four segments are live (Phase 3 onward, Section 2.2a) — at launch, the mix is intentionally 100% Home Care.**

### 2.5 Where Holiday Rentals Fit
Target property managers, not individual Airbnb hosts. Position as "professionally managed property presentation and turnover services for premium holiday accommodation," targeting luxury apartments, penthouses, waterfront homes, large holiday homes, and premium property/Airbnb management companies. Avoid cheap studios, low-ADR properties, and same-day-turnaround demands.

If pursued at scale, operate this as a distinct internal division — "Vantage Point Stay" — selling property operations (cleaning, linen, consumables, damage reporting, photo verification, guest-ready certification), not just cleaning.

### 2.6 Pricing
**The published package ladder is the operative pricing reference** — `docs/residential-package-and-pricing-model.md`. Essential and Signature are sold at published flat rates with instant quoting; Estate Care is walkthrough-quoted from $2,000 per visit. Pricing is per visit and uniform across all five zones.

Every rung derives from one room model — `0.55h base + 0.35h/bedroom + 0.50h/bathroom + 0.30h/extra living area` — priced at **$130 per labour hour**: a **$65/hour charge-out floor at a 2× multiple**. Minimum job price $199.

**Cost basis:** cleaners are paid **$50/hour** — above the Cleaning Services Award band of $26–33 (§4.1) and above typical Gold Coast cleaner pay of $30–40. Labour is therefore **38.5% of revenue**, gross margin $80/hour, and the multiple over the cleaner rate is **2.6×**. The $65 floor is absolute: no package may be priced below it.

| Tier | Entry | 3 bed / 2 bath | 5 bed / 4 bath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essential | $199 | $379 | $639 |
| Signature | $349 | $569 | $879 |
| Estate Care | $2,000+ | walkthrough only | walkthrough only |

**Legacy internal bands**, retained for sanity-checking only and no longer operative: Premium $300–500+, Luxury $600–1,500+, Estate/concierge $1,500–3,000+, all per visit. Note these band names never mapped to the tier names — Signature at 5 bed/3 bath ($799) sits inside the "Luxury" band while remaining a Home Care tier.

**On the 3× question.** A 3× multiple over a $70/hour wage is not achievable at premium-market prices — it would put the 3-bed/2-bath at $604, two to four times the verified Gold Coast market and above §4.4's own premium ceiling. At $50/hour and $130/hour pricing the achieved multiple is **2.6×**. Prices can rise later once the business is established without touching the cost base.

### 2.8 Revenue Conversion Logic
The core KPI is recurring MRR per customer, not one-off job value. A $699 Deep Reset used as an acquisition product, converted to a $349 fortnightly recurring plan, is worth roughly **$9,074/year** (26 fortnights), not $699.

Note what that example implies for planning: an Essential 3 bed/2 bath plan at $379 fortnightly is a plan value of $821/month and $9,854 a year. Scenario assumptions in Section 10.2 are anchored to that reality.

Sales motion: one-off clean → exceptional experience → follow-up → "would you like us to take care of this on an ongoing basis?" → recurring plan.

## 4. Market Analysis

### 4.1 Industry Overview
Australia's cleaning services industry generates approximately $20 billion in annual revenue, growing at a compound annual rate of 5–7% over the past five years. It is large, fragmented, and structurally resilient. More than 80% of businesses in the industry have fewer than 20 employees — Vantage Point's target scale aligns with this dominant structure. Demand for eco-friendly products and enhanced hygiene protocols has increased steadily since the pandemic and is a genuine differentiator with premium residential clients. Most of the workforce is covered by the Cleaning Services Award, with average hourly rates from the mid-$20s to $30. Wage non-compliance has been found in a significant share of audited businesses — reinforcing the Fair Work compliance steps in Section 3.9.

### 4.2 Competitive Landscape
Existing local providers — Cleaner Space Australia, Gold Coast Cleaning Agency, Gold Coast Cleans, and similar — compete in the generic residential market on price and general reliability. Nearly every reviewed competitor markets on the same core promises — reliability, punctuality, responsiveness — with no structured, data-backed proof of quality. None position clearly as a premium, managed home care service; trust is built on relationship and manual communication, not data or presentation standard. This confirms a genuine, verified gap: Vantage Point's premium positioning and SafetyCulture-based inspection reporting are real differentiators in this local market.

### 4.3 Competitive Advantage
Most local competitors compete on price and claimed reliability in the standard residential segment Vantage Point deliberately avoids (Section 2.2). Vantage Point demonstrates consistent quality through rigorous inspections, digital checklists, and transparent reporting, letting clients see service standards for themselves. Scalable systems (Volumes 2 and 4) maintain these standards as the business grows without a proportional rise in administrative overhead. A specific, measurable edge: every inbound call and chat is answered live, 24/7, by the GHL AI Voice Agent (Volume 4, Section 2.5a), which captures the enquiry and books a private home care assessment against live calendar availability on the spot — never quoting a price, consistent with Section 5.3. None of the direct competitors identified in the Zones 1–5 market analysis offer anything comparable. This isn't just a service-quality point: leads contacted within five minutes are roughly 21× more likely to qualify than those left half an hour, and 78% of buyers go with whichever business responds first (Lead Response Management Study; MIT/Harvard Business Review) — an after-hours enquiry that would otherwise be lost becomes a booked job instead.

### 4.4 Gold Coast Residential Pricing Benchmark
| Service | Budget | Market average | High-ticket / Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly rate | $35–45/hr | $50–65/hr | $75–120+/hr |
| Weekly/fortnightly maintenance | $100–180 | $180–280 | $280–500+ |
| Standard 3-bed, 2-bath | $140–180 | $180–280 | $300–450 |
| Standard 4-bed, 2-bath | $180–250 | $250–350 | $400–650 |
| Deep clean | $250–400 | $400–650 | $700–1,200+ |
| Luxury home (400m²+) | $350–500 | $500–800 | $800–2,000+ |

Current Gold Coast market averages sit around $50–70/hour, with standard house cleans typically $120–$315 depending on size and condition — reinforcing that the premium tier (Section 2.6) is a deliberate positioning choice, not the market default. Vantage Point positions deliberately in the third column (Volume 3 for packaging).

## 5. Website & Booking Technology

### 5.1 Lead Capture & Booking Flow
The website captures leads through a CRM form requesting property details — address, property size/type, service tier of interest, preferred frequency — rather than a generic contact form. Submission triggers a private home care assessment booking flow, with available assessment times pulled from the GoHighLevel native calendar so the prospect books directly against real availability rather than waiting on a callback.

### 5.2 Flow
- Website form (property details) → CRM captures and scores the lead.
- Prospect selects an assessment time (pulled live from the GoHighLevel calendar).
- Automated confirmation and reminder sequence.
- Walkthrough delivered → proposal issued → follow-up sequence.
- Conversion to recurring plan (Section 2.8's revenue conversion logic).

### 5.3 Positioning in the Flow — Published Prices, Bounded Scope
The call-to-action is **"Request Your Private Home Care Assessment"** for Estate Care. Essential and Signature lead with a published price and a booking.

**This section previously forbade showing any price before an assessment, and was authoritative across the suite. That decision has been reversed.** Essential and Signature now publish flat rates and quote instantly from customer-declared property details. Estate Care alone retains the walkthrough path, at a $100 fee credited against the first clean.

**What the reversal costs, stated plainly.** The competitor analysis defines the market's own positioning axis: *Value* competitors compete on "affordability/instant online pricing"; *Premium* competitors compete on "trust, presentation, discretion — price not the lead message." Every premium competitor across Zones 1–5 publishes no pricing. Publishing rates moves this brand toward the Value quadrant on that axis, and the prices themselves do not offset it — at $379 the 3-bed/2-bath is 1.20× the verified market ceiling, and the 4-bed at $419 is 1.02×. These are top-of-band prices, not obviously-premium ones.

**What must carry the premium instead:** the bounded inclusions (§2.3), the same-cleaner-every-visit promise, and the SafetyCulture inspection record — the one thing the competitor analysis found no competitor in this market provides. If those are weak, the pricing has nothing behind it.

**What the reversal requires.** Because a price is issued before anyone visits, the customer's declared property details are the pricing basis. That makes two things mandatory rather than optional: a timestamped Terms & Conditions acknowledgement at form submission, and OPS-16 declared-scope variance handling on site (Volume 2). See `docs/scope-and-variance-protection.md`.

Full field-by-field form specification and the CRM automation build are detailed in Volume 3.

## 9. Hiring & Contract Acquisition Plan

### 9.1 Workforce Model & Cleaner Pay
Vantage Point launches on vetted, fully insured subcontractors for the first 6–12 months. Quality control is maintained through inspection outcomes against the CLN service standards (QUA-01), not through direct supervision alone. Direct employment begins once revenue is predictable enough to carry fixed payroll (Section 6.2), not at a fixed calendar milestone.

**Cleaner rate: $50 per hour.** Deliberately above the Cleaning Services Award band of $26–33 (Section 4.1) and above typical Gold Coast cleaner pay of roughly $30–40. Paying above market is what makes the same-cleaner-every-visit promise deliverable — it is a retention mechanism, and retention is what the brand actually sells.

At the published pricing basis of $130 per labour hour (Section 2.6), labour is **38.5% of revenue**.

> **⚠️ This replaces the previous 50% profit-split model, and the change is not merely arithmetic.** A fixed hourly rate is a **stronger employment indicium than a profit share** — a genuinely independent contractor prices their own work. Combined with the same-cleaner promise, central rostering, company service standards, uniforms and company inspection (Volume 2 §2.12), this materially increases the classification exposure recorded as audit item O1.
>
> **Get employment-law advice before engaging the first cleaner on this basis.** The commercial model may be right; the engagement structure needs to be deliberate rather than inherited. Options include engaging cleaners as employees from the outset and pricing accordingly, or restructuring the rate as a genuine per-job fee the contractor accepts or declines.

**Pricing floor — check this against every plan won.** No package may be priced below **$65 per labour hour** (Section 2.6). At a $50/hour cleaner rate, that floor is what keeps the job profitable at all; the published ladder clears it at roughly 2×. Discounting below the floor does not just reduce margin, it inverts it once supplies and overhead are counted.

The standby bench (hiring ahead of confirmed demand) is deliberate: it exists so a newly won plan can be staffed immediately rather than triggering a fresh recruitment cycle, protecting the ability to close and start quickly.

### 10.3 Reading the Forecast
The planning case is **$110,332** — Home Care-only pricing, the Section 9.2 acquisition pace, and 5% monthly churn. Even the Upside case ($148,072) does not reach the $250,000 target this roadmap previously carried.

**What $250,000 would actually require**, at Premium-band pricing:

| New plans/month | Conservative | Planning | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 (Section 9.2 pace) | $75k | $110k | $148k |
| 3.0 | $106k | $155k | $208k |
| **4.5** | $151k | $221k | **$268k** |

Roughly **4.5 new plans every month for twelve months at the top of the Premium band** — three times the Section 9.2 pace, reaching 30 active plans by Month 12. That is not a stretch of this plan; it is a different plan, and no acquisition strategy in this suite produces it. Note also that Section 9.2's pace is not crew-constrained — the constraint is lead flow and founder selling time (Section 6.3), which is precisely what Phase 10 of the tech-stack plan defers paid marketing against.

The alternative route to $250,000 — higher plan values rather than more plans — requires Luxury-band pricing, which Section 2.2a gates behind 20 Home Care plans, which at any realistic pace does not clear until Year 2. That circularity is why the target was retired rather than reduced.

**The lever that matters most in Year 1 is retention, not acquisition.** Cutting churn from 5% to 3% adds ~$10,000 with no additional selling; adding 1.5 plans/month adds ~$45,000 but requires doubling lead flow. Both are worth doing; only one is cheap.

All figures are planning estimates. Revisit this model with actual Month 1–3 data as soon as it exists — particularly churn, which is currently an assumption rather than a measurement.
