## 1. Introduction
This manual translates residential strategy (Volume 1) into who does what, day to day. A role is not created until Volume 1's delegation triggers say the business can afford it, and no role runs on tribal knowledge — every recurring task has a numbered SOP.

**Principles:**
- One owner per SOP — not "the team."
- Write it before you need it twice. The second time a task repeats, it gets an SOP number, even in draft form.
- SOPs are stored where the work happens — field-facing SOPs live in Connecteam; client- and pipeline-facing SOPs live in GoHighLevel; the master library lives in a GitHub repository as the single source of truth (Volume 4).
- A role is not created ahead of the funding trigger that supports it.

> **Note:** The residential SOP library below is built from the shared operating framework and is at an earlier stage of completion than the org structure. Several residential-specific SOPs (e.g. the full residential onboarding sequence) are indexed but not yet written out in full — write these out the first time the owning role is hired or the task repeats a second time, per Section 1.2 of the original framework.

## 2. Organisation Structure
Every role carries the same fields — responsibilities, KPIs, SOP ownership, technology ownership, decision authority.

### 2.1 Founder & CEO
Active from day one. **Responsibilities:** strategy & vision, business development & high-value sales, partnerships, financial management, technology & AI architecture, hiring leaders, growth. Not responsible for cleaning, scheduling, invoicing, payroll, customer support, or routine admin. **KPIs:** revenue vs. target, net profit margin, MRR growth, CAC:LTV ratio. **SOP ownership:** all SOPs until delegated; retains Strategy, Finance, AI long-term. **Technology ownership:** full stack architecture, GitHub admin. **Decision authority:** final on pricing, hiring, capital allocation, strategy.

### 2.2 Virtual Assistant (VA)
Activates once revenue covers running costs + subcontractors, sustained two months. **Responsibilities:** lead pre-screening, calendar/inbox triage, scheduling support, data entry, applicant document collection. **KPIs:** lead response time, admin backlog cleared same-day, scheduling error rate. **Technology:** GoHighLevel (inbox, calendar), Google Workspace. **Decision authority:** none on pricing/hiring; escalates anything outside a documented SOP.

### 2.3 Cleaning Supervisor / Field Lead
Activates once MRR consistency is proven. **Responsibilities:** field inspections, quality checks (SafetyCulture), new subcontractor/cleaner induction, first-line WHS incident response, crew rostering support. **KPIs:** inspection pass rate, complaint/callback rate, on-time arrival rate, induction completion rate. **Technology:** SafetyCulture, Connecteam (field module). **Decision authority:** can stand down a subcontractor from a shift for compliance/safety failure; cannot terminate a contract.

### 2.4 Operations Manager
Activates once the Cleaning Supervisor layer is proven. **Responsibilities:** scheduling and dispatch, compliance oversight, staff performance management, escalation point for field issues, SOP maintenance (Operations and Cleaning categories). **KPIs:** labour cost %, unbilled hours, job profitability, staff turnover, SOP currency. **Technology:** Connecteam (full), GoHighLevel (dispatch). **Decision authority:** rostering, subcontractor engagement/disengagement, operational spend within budget.

### 2.5 Sales Manager / BDM
Activates in the Scale stage. **Responsibilities:** inbound/outbound pipeline management, quoting, home care plan conversion, partnership relationship management (real estate/property managers). **KPIs:** assessment-to-plan conversion, average plan value, pipeline velocity, partnership-sourced revenue. **Technology:** GoHighLevel (sales pipelines). **Decision authority:** quoting within approved pricing bands; escalates outside-band pricing to CEO.

### 2.6 Marketing Manager
Activates in the Scale stage. **Responsibilities:** campaign management, funnel performance, content, SEO/local search, review-generation program. **KPIs:** CAC, CPL, CTR, conversion rate, ROAS, ROI. **Technology:** Google Ads, GA4, Tag Manager, Search Console, GoHighLevel automations. **Decision authority:** campaign spend within approved marketing allocation.

### 2.7 HR Lead
Activates as employee headcount grows past what ad hoc handling can sustain. **Responsibilities:** recruitment coordination, onboarding, employee records, award interpretation, performance framework, culture/engagement. **KPIs:** time-to-hire, turnover, onboarding completion, engagement survey results. **Technology:** Employment Hero. **Decision authority:** hiring recommendation for non-leadership roles; final decision with CEO until General Manager stage.

### 2.8 Finance Lead / Controller
Activates at the Enterprise stage, earlier if complexity warrants. **Responsibilities:** full P&L ownership, cash flow forecasting, payroll oversight, pricing governance support, board/investor reporting. **KPIs:** net profit margin, DSO, cash position vs. forecast, budget variance. **Technology:** Xero (full), Stripe. **Decision authority:** approves opex within budget; flags waterfall deviations to CEO.

### 2.9 Quality Assurance Supervisor
Activates in the Scale stage. **Responsibilities:** field inspections and client audits (SafetyCulture), inspection data analysis, corrective action tracking. **KPIs:** inspection pass rate, repeat-issue rate, client audit satisfaction. **Technology:** SafetyCulture. **Decision authority:** can flag a site/crew for retraining; cannot action HR consequences.

### 2.10 General Manager
Activates at the Enterprise stage. **Responsibilities:** runs day-to-day operations across all departments, freeing the CEO for capital allocation and expansion. **KPIs:** consolidated business KPI set, department-head performance. **Technology:** executive dashboard. **Decision authority:** full operational authority; capital allocation and equity/exit decisions remain with CEO.

### 2.11 Direct Service Delivery — Cleaner (Employee)
**Responsibilities:** delivers the scheduled clean to SOP standard, completes SafetyCulture checklists on site, reports issues/damage immediately, represents the brand in the client's home. **KPIs:** job completion rate, inspection pass rate, punctuality, client complaint rate. **Technology:** Connecteam, SafetyCulture. **Decision authority:** none on pricing/scheduling/scope — escalates to Cleaning Supervisor.

### 2.12 Subcontractor (Non-Employee Relationship)

> **⚠️ Unresolved — open item O1. Get employment-law advice before engaging the first subcontractor.**
> This role definition and the business model contradict each other. Independence here requires the right to delegate; Volume 1 §2.3 and the website's lead trust point promise the client **the same cleaner every visit**. Those cannot both hold. Around them sit central rostering (OPS-02), a uniform and presentation standard (CLN-20), company service standards (CLN-21/22), company inspection (QUA-01), and a fixed 50% split — conventional indicia of control.
> COM-05 and COM-11 treat this as a risk to review periodically. It is better understood as a design decision to make deliberately: either the marketing promise changes, or the engagement model does, or the exposure is accepted with advice. Volume 1 §9.2 also sets a per-visit pricing floor below which the effective hourly rate stops being defensible.

Genuinely independent business engaged under a subcontractor agreement — not an employee. Must hold own ABN, own equipment, own insurance, and retain the right to delegate. Must provide Certificate of Currency ($10M PL residential, naming their exact ABN business name), active ABN, signed Subcontractor Agreement, and current police check/clearance before the first job — police checks are especially important given subcontractors hold client keys and unsupervised home access. Performs to the same CLN and QUA SOP standards as employees, under their own operating methods and equipment. Engagement/disengagement authority: Cleaning Supervisor / Operations Manager. Compliance owner: CEO / Finance Lead (TPAR reporting, sham-contracting risk review, cleaner rate administration per Volume 1, Section 9.1).

## 5. Priority SOPs — Full Text
These fourteen SOPs carry legal, financial, or safety consequences if done inconsistently. Every other SOP follows this same structure when written out.

### COM-01 — Chemical & Hazardous Substances (SDS) Register
**Owner:** CEO. **Related:** COM-02, CLN-18, EMR-05.
**Purpose:** Ensure every chemical used in any client's home has a current Safety Data Sheet on file and accessible to the person using it.
**Procedure:**
1. Before any new chemical is introduced, obtain its current SDS from the manufacturer/supplier.
2. Log the chemical in the master SDS register (product name, supplier, hazard class, storage requirement, first-aid measures).
3. Upload the SDS PDF to the shared register and link it in Connecteam for field access.
4. Confirm storage meets the SDS requirement.
5. Review the full register quarterly.
6. If a client requests a specific chemical not on the register (e.g. for allergies or pets), obtain its SDS before use and log it as a client-specific addendum.
7. In the event of exposure or spill, refer immediately to EMR-05.

### COM-02 — Subcontractor Compliance Document Collection
**Owner:** CEO / Operations Manager. **Related:** REC-01, COM-05, COM-11.
**Purpose:** Ensure no subcontractor is assigned a job — and given a client's home access — before the four required compliance documents are collected and verified.
**Procedure:**
1. Request before any job is assigned: Certificate of Currency ($10M PL residential, naming the subcontractor's exact ABN business name), active ABN confirmation, a signed Subcontractor Agreement, and a police check/clearance less than 12–24 months old.
2. Verify the ABN is active via the ABR lookup.
3. Verify the CoC's named insured matches the ABN business name exactly.
4. File all four documents with an expiry-tracking date for the CoC and police check.
5. Do not release any job — and never issue keys or access codes — until all four documents show verified.
6. Set an automated reminder 30 days before expiry; suspend assignment if it lapses.
7. Annually, cross-check the working pattern against the COM-11 classification checklist.

### EMR-01 — Emergency Response — Client Home Incident
**Owner:** CEO / Operations Manager. **Related:** EMR-02, EMR-05, EMR-06, BCP-01.
**Purpose:** Ensure anyone facing an emergency in a client's home knows exactly what to do without needing to find this document mid-incident.
**Procedure:**
1. If there is immediate danger to life, call 000 first.
2. Once addressed, call the Cleaning Supervisor (business hours) or CEO (after hours).
3. Do not move an injured person unless in further danger; do not admit fault or discuss liability.
4. If safe, photograph the scene before anything is cleaned up or moved.
5. Notify the homeowner/client as soon as practicable.
6. Complete the SafetyCulture incident report within 24 hours, regardless of severity.
7. For injury, follow EMR-02; chemical incident, EMR-05; vehicle incident, EMR-06.
8. CEO reviews every incident report within 48 hours and updates the relevant SOP if a gap is revealed.

### OPS-02 — Recurring Clean Scheduling & Dispatch
**Owner:** Operations Manager (**CEO at launch, then VA**). **Related:** OPS-04, VEH-06.
**Purpose:** Keep every recurring residential clean scheduled, staffed, and dispatched without gaps, protecting the unbilled-hours and client-retention KPIs.
**Procedure:**
1. Maintain the rolling four-week schedule in Connecteam.
2. Run a coverage check every Friday for the following week; escalate unassigned jobs before Monday.
3. Confirm zone-based route efficiency (Volume 1, Section 2.7) for staff covering multiple homes in a day before finalising the roster.
4. Notify the client only if a scheduling change affects their agreed day/time — routine crew substitutions within the same standard do not require client notice.
5. Log any missed/failed clean immediately and trigger OPS-06 recovery.
6. Reconcile completed jobs against schedule weekly for Labour Cost % and job profitability.

### OPS-13 — 24/7 Voice & Chat Lead Capture and Issue Escalation
**Owner:** CEO (Operations Manager once hired). **Related:** OPS-03, OPS-05, OPS-16, Volume 4 GHL Voice Agent (Section 2.5a) and Website Chatbot (Section 2.5b).
**Purpose:** Ensure no enquiry — new lead or existing-client issue — is ever lost to voicemail or an unanswered chat, at any hour. Speed to first response is the single biggest lever on whether a lead converts at all: businesses that respond within five minutes qualify roughly 21× more leads than those that wait thirty, and 78% of customers buy from whichever business responds first (Lead Response Management Study; MIT/Harvard Business Review). This SOP is what makes that response instant, 24/7, rather than dependent on someone being near a phone.
**Procedure:**
1. Every inbound call to the GHL local phone number, and every website chat, is answered live by the AI Voice Agent/chatbot whenever a human isn't immediately available — including 100% of after-hours contact.
2. For a **new lead**: the agent captures name, email, and mobile number at minimum, plus service interest and property details where the caller is ready to provide them, and writes this directly into the CRM contact record, tagged and routed into the Residential pipeline (Volume 3, Section 2.1) — automatically, with no manual re-entry step.
3. For an Essential or Signature enquiry, the agent captures the property details in Volume 3 Section 5.1, returns the **published package price** from the ladder, captures the T&Cs acknowledgement verbally into the transcript, and books the first clean against live GoHighLevel calendar availability. **The agent quotes from the published ladder only** — never a derived, discounted or discretionary figure, and never for Estate Care, properties beyond 5 bed / 4 bath, or any property whose condition is rated High. Those route to a human. This guardrail is adversarially tested before go-live (tech-stack plan, Phase 9).
4. For an **existing client reporting an issue**: the agent matches the caller against the CRM, acknowledges the issue within the call/chat itself, and logs a full transcript against the client's account.
5. Any call/chat containing an escalation trigger word (emergency, complaint, dispute, injury) is transferred live to the CEO's/on-call mobile immediately — never queued for next-business-day contact.
6. Every captured lead is followed up by a human within the same business day, even where the agent has already booked a job — this is a courtesy check-in, not a re-sell, and catches anything the agent's automated flow didn't fully resolve.
7. Weekly review of call/chat transcripts and outcomes (answer rate, booking-completion rate, escalations) feeds the KPI dashboard (Volume 4, Section 4) and flags any pattern of the agent mishandling a query type, which triggers an update to its FAQ/pricing reference document rather than waiting for a client complaint to surface the gap.
**Related Documents & Tools:**
- GHL Voice Agent / Website Chatbot configuration (Volume 4, Section 2.5a/2.5b)
- Property intake form and T&Cs (Volume 3, Section 5.1)
- Residential pipeline (Volume 3, Section 2.1)

### OPS-16 — On-Site Scope Variance Verification
**Owner:** Cleaning Supervisor (**crew lead, or CEO, pre-hire**). **Related:** OPS-02, QUA-01, FIN-01, COM (Terms & Conditions — Condition Variance clause).
**Purpose:** Give crews a clear, non-judgment-call process for handling a property that does not match the scope its instant quote was based on, so the customer conversation is consistent regardless of who is on site, and the variance is documented well enough to hold up if disputed later.

**This SOP is what makes instant quoting survivable.** Essential and Signature are priced from the customer's own declaration before anyone visits (Volume 3, Section 5.1). Where that declaration is wrong — an undeclared room, a materially larger property, a condition well beyond a maintenance clean — this is the mechanism that stops the business absorbing the cost. It reverted to declared-scope handling in August 2026 when instant quoting was reinstated.

**Scope:** Applies to every Essential and Signature booking, all of which are quoted from self-declared details. Does **not** apply to Estate Care, which is walkthrough-quoted and therefore physically verified before a price is issued.

**Not a licence to upcharge.** The threshold is objective (below), the conversation happens before extra work starts, and the customer always has a no-extra-cost option. If this SOP fires often, the quote form is asking the wrong questions — fix the form, not the invoice.

**Procedure:**
1. On arrival, the crew lead does a two-minute walkthrough against the declared property details on the booking, before starting work.
2. If actual scope materially exceeds the declaration — **more than 10–15% above declared floor area, one or more entirely undeclared rooms or serviceable spaces, or condition rated Low/Medium at booking but plainly requiring a reset** — the crew lead photographs the discrepancy and logs it in Connecteam against the job.
3. The crew lead — or the office, where a VA/Operations Manager exists — contacts the customer by phone, then SMS, then email if unreachable, explaining the variance in plain terms and presenting the two Terms & Conditions options: (a) extend at $100/hour above the quoted time, or (b) keep the fixed price and time cap, work completed in priority order, remaining areas left undone as agreed.
4. The customer's choice is confirmed in writing (SMS/email reply or verbal confirmation logged in Connecteam) before work proceeds past the point of variance.
5. If unreachable after all three contact attempts within approximately 15 minutes, default to Option (b) and log the attempted-contact timestamps in the job record.
6. For Option (a): extension time is tracked from the agreed start, billed in 30-minute increments, and confirmed on the invoice as a separate line item from the original fixed price (FIN-01).
7. For Option (b): the crew works the pre-agreed priority order (standard order per service tier, or the customer's stated preference if captured at booking) and notes any uncompleted areas on the job completion report shared with the customer.
8. Every variance instance is logged for trend review. A second material variance at the same address triggers the **Repeat Variance** provision in the Terms & Conditions — require an in-person or video property assessment before confirming further instant-quote bookings for that address.
**Related Documents & Tools:**
- Declared property details on the booking (Volume 3, Section 5.1)
- Terms & Conditions — Property Scope & Fixed-Price Variance clause
- Connecteam (variance logging, customer confirmation record)

**A note on frequency.** A rising variance rate means the quote form is under-capturing, not that customers are dishonest. Feed every instance back into the Section 5.1 field list. Treat it as a form-design signal, never as a revenue line.

### QUA-01 — Site Inspection via SafetyCulture
**Owner:** Quality Assurance Supervisor (Cleaning Supervisor once hired; **CEO at launch**). **Related:** QUA-03, QUA-09.
**Launch note:** this SOP produces the inspection data that Volume 1 §4.2–4.3 names as the company's single competitive differentiator, and the competitor analysis confirms no competitor in Zones 1–5 publishes anything comparable. It cannot wait for the Scale stage. The CEO runs it from the first job.
**Purpose:** Verify service quality objectively and consistently, generating the inspection data that is a core competitive differentiator.
**Procedure:**
1. Open the correct SafetyCulture checklist template for the service tier (Essential, Signature, or Estate Care — QUA-09).
2. Walk the home systematically, checking every area against the checklist.
3. Photograph any failed item as evidence.
4. Score and submit the inspection immediately on-site.
5. For any failed item, log a corrective action (QUA-03) with an owner and re-check date.
6. Escalate repeat failures at the same home to retraining consideration (QUA-06).
