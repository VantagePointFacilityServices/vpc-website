# Vantage Point Cleaning — Residential Package & Pricing Model
## Essential · Signature · Estate Care | Instant quoting, Zones 1–5
**Status: active. Supersedes the assessment-first pricing decision of the August 2026 audit (finding S2).**

---

## 1. The decision

Essential and Signature are sold as **published flat-rate packages with instant quoting**. Estate Care is **excluded from instant quoting** and reached only through a paid walkthrough.

| | Essential | Signature | Estate Care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instant quote | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| Price published | Yes | Yes | No |
| Pricing basis | Customer-declared property | Customer-declared property | Walkthrough |
| Entry | $199 | $349 | $2,000+ per visit |
| Path to price | Web form, chatbot, or voice agent | Same | $100 walkthrough, credited on booking |

Pricing is **per visit** and **uniform across all five zones** (Vol 1 §2.7). Zone-differentiated published pricing reads as opportunistic and cannot be defended on a website.

### What this reverses, and what follows from it

The August 2026 audit resolved the suite to assessment-first, no price shown, on the evidence that instant online pricing is a Value-quadrant signal (`residential-direct-competitor-market-analysis.md`, Positioning Axis) and that no premium competitor in Zones 1–5 publishes pricing. That analysis has not changed; the commercial decision has. Finding S2 remains in the audit, marked superseded, because the reasoning may be worth revisiting.

Two consequences are load-bearing:

1. **Self-declared scope is the pricing basis again.** A price is issued before anyone visits the property, so OPS-16 reverts from condition variance to declared-scope variance, and the mandatory timestamped T&Cs acknowledgement returns to form submission.
2. **The price cannot carry the positioning.** At $379 the 3-bed/2-bath is 1.20× the verified market ceiling; the 4-bed at $419 is 1.02×. These are top-of-normal-band prices, not obviously-premium prices. The inclusions in §6, the same-cleaner promise, and the SafetyCulture inspection record now carry the entire premium claim. If those are weak, the pricing has nothing behind it.

## 3. The pricing model

Every price derives from one room model rather than from stepping round numbers. This is what keeps the base ladder and the increments in agreement.

**Labour hours** = `0.55h base` + `0.35h × bedrooms` + `0.50h × bathrooms` + `0.30h × (living areas − 1)`

The base covers kitchen, entry, hallways, bins, high-touch surfaces and general floors. Living areas are assumed at 1 for studio–2 bed, 2 for 3–4 bed, 3 for 5 bed.

**Price** = labour hours × **$130**

### Where $130 comes from
| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Business charge-out **floor** | **$65** per labour hour |
| Package pricing multiple | **2×** the floor |
| **Pricing basis** | **$130** per labour hour |
| Cleaner rate | **$50** per hour |
| Labour as % of revenue | **38.5%** |
| Gross margin | **$80** per labour hour · **61.5%** |
| Multiple over cleaner rate | **2.6×** |

$65/hour is the **floor** — no package may ever be priced below it. Every rung currently clears it at 1.98–2.50×, and the floor is what protects the ladder from being discounted into unprofitability later.

**The cleaner rate is $50/hour** — above the Cleaning Services Award band of $26–33 (Vol 1 §4.1) and above typical Gold Coast cleaner pay of roughly $30–40. Paying above market is what makes the same-cleaner-every-visit promise deliverable; it is a retention strategy, not generosity.

> **⚠️ This is an hourly rate, not the 50% profit split in Vol 1 §9.1.** Two things follow. First, the workforce model in §9.1 needs restating — labour is now 38.5% of revenue, not 50%, which materially improves the Year 1 model (Vol 1 §10.2). Second, and more seriously, **paying a fixed hourly rate is a stronger employment indicium than a profit share.** Audit item O1 — the sham-contracting exposure — gets *worse* under this model, not better. Get employment-law advice before engaging the first cleaner on this basis.

### On the 3× question
An earlier round targeted a 3× multiple over a $70/hour wage. That is not reachable at premium-market prices — it would put the 3-bed/2-bath at $604, two to four times the verified Gold Coast market. At $50/hour and $130/hour pricing the achieved multiple is **2.6×**, which is close, and prices can rise later once the business is established without touching the cost base.

**Minimum job price: $199.** Below roughly 1.5 hours a job cannot carry its own travel and setup.

## 7. First visit

**Charged at 2× the package price**, disclosed at booking.

A first clean takes two to three times an ongoing visit — the crew is bringing the home to a baseline rather than maintaining one. On a flat rate that would pay the subcontractor $18–27/hour, below the Award band and squarely inside the classification exposure flagged as audit item O1.

| Time overrun | Sustained wage on a 2× first-visit price |
|---|---|
| 2.0× | $65/hr — fully covered, exactly at the floor |
| 2.5× | $52/hr — below the floor, but above cleaner cost |
| 3.0× | $43/hr — below the $50 cleaner rate. Loss-making. |

**At a genuine 3× overrun, do not accept the booking onto a plan.** Route it to a quoted Deep Reset (Vol 1 §2.8's acquisition product), then convert to a recurring plan afterwards. The quoter must ask a property-condition question and gate on it.

## 8. Estate Care — walkthrough only

Not instant quoted. **$100 walkthrough fee, credited in full against the first clean on booking.** Custom quotes from **$2,000 per visit**.

**Entry is defined by service model, not property size.** Estate Care means a dedicated team, supervisor inspection every visit, a custom cleaning program, concierge contact and priority scheduling. The gap between Signature `5 bed + 4 bath` ($879) and Estate ($2,000+) is intentional and should not be filled: a large house is a Signature build-up with increments, an estate is a different service.

The $100 fee reverses the "free walkthrough" language in Vol 1 and the landing-page brief. It filters enquiries on the most expensive-to-serve segment while costing a genuine buyer nothing.

**Estate Care remains gated to Phase 3** under Vol 1 §2.2a — build the page, do not publish it until Luxury Homes is proven.

## 9. Package inclusions

**The governing rule: an inclusion that is not bounded cannot be flat-rated.** "Laundry" is unbounded — one household has two loads, another has eight. Every inclusion below carries an explicit boundary, and those boundaries are what make the flat rate survivable. They are also the specification CLN-21 and CLN-22 have been missing (audit item O6).

### Essential — every visit
- **Kitchen** — benches, splashback, sink, cooktop, appliance exteriors, microwave interior
- **Bathrooms** — shower, bath, vanity, mirror, toilet, tiles spot-treated
- **Floors** — all vacuumed, hard floors mopped
- **Dusting** — reachable surfaces, skirtings, sills
- **Beds made** — linen not changed
- **Bins** — emptied and relined
- **High-touch sanitising** — switches, handles, remotes
- **Same cleaner or team**, every visit
- **SafetyCulture inspection** logged against the job
- **Before-and-after photographs**

### Signature — everything in Essential, plus
- **Linen changed on all beds** — linen supplied by the client
- **One laundry load** — washed, dried, folded
- **Interior glass at reachable height** — ground floor and accessible upper windows
- **Detailed kitchen** — appliance fronts, splashback detail, cupboard fronts
- **One rotational deep-clean task per visit** — from the published schedule below
- **Consumable monitoring** with a restock list
- **Priority booking window**

### The rotational schedule
This is the mechanism that makes "deep clean" bounded and predictable. One task per visit, eight-visit cycle:

`oven interior` → `fridge interior` → `interior windows` → `skirtings & doors` → `blinds` → `cupboard fronts` → `entry & outdoor` → `light fittings`

At fortnightly frequency the full cycle completes in roughly four months. The customer can see where they are in the cycle, which turns a vague promise into a visible program.

### Estate Care
Everything in Signature, unbounded and set at the walkthrough, plus: dedicated team · supervisor inspection every visit · custom cleaning program · rotational maintenance to a bespoke schedule · home presentation · priority scheduling · concierge-style single point of contact.

### Excluded from all tiers — available as priced add-ons
Oven interior outside the rotation · fridge interior outside the rotation · upper-level exterior glass · garage beyond the increment · additional laundry loads · pet-hair treatment · wall washing · carpet steam cleaning · post-party or post-renovation reset.

## 10. Zone strategy

**Pricing is uniform.** Zones determine which tier to lead with, not what to charge. Per the competitor analysis §3:

| Zone | Property stock | Lead tier | Competitive note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Burleigh, Miami, Mermaid, Broadbeach | Mixed apartments and houses | Essential volume | Most branded competition of any zone |
| 2 · Palm Beach, Tugun, Currumbin | Canal-front and beach houses | Signature | Land Based Cleaning is the luxury incumbent |
| 3 · Robina, Varsity Lakes, Mudgeeraba, Reedy Creek | Family homes | Essential density | Least brand competition — lowest resistance |
| 4 · Main Beach, Surfers, Bundall, Broadbeach Waters | High-rise and waterfront | Signature; Estate later | The Gold Standard Co. present; highest-value stock |
| 5 · Paradise Point, Runaway Bay, Hope Island, Sanctuary Cove | Gated estates, waterfront | Estate later | Bloom Clean is the incumbent to watch |

**The apartment rungs are worth a deliberate push.** Studio–2 bed configs concentrate in Zones 1 and 4, where high-rise density makes route efficiency best — and `2 bed + 2 bath` at 1.23× market is the ladder's strongest premium. Zone 4 in particular pairs the best margin with the best travel economics.

**Travel is not in the pricing model.** At 0.4h of unbilled travel and setup per job, the real sustained wage falls to $56–61/hr. The $199 minimum protects small jobs; nothing protects a scattered run. This is the strongest financial argument for the zone-density strategy in Vol 1 §2.7 — density is worth roughly $5/hour.

## 11. What the quote form must ask

To produce a price: **bedrooms · bathrooms · powder rooms · living areas · tier**. Everything else is an increment.

To protect the price: **property condition** (gates the first-visit decision, §7) · **multi-level** · **outdoor areas, pool, balconies** · **study, media room, walk-in robes, garage**.

To make the price binding: **mandatory, timestamped T&Cs acknowledgement** confirming the declared details are accurate and that the scope-variance clause applies if the property materially differs — see `scope-and-variance-protection.md`.

**Non-standard requests route to a human**, not to a price: more than 5 bedrooms, more than 4 bathrooms, condition rated High, or any Estate-level enquiry.
